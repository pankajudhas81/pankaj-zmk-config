# Keycap Buying Guide — Corne in Bangalore

> **This doc is about what to actually buy and where.** Theory (profiles, materials, legend methods) lives in [keys-research.md](./keys-research.md). Read that first if you want the foundations. This doc assumes you've absorbed it and just want to make a purchase.

---

## TL;DR for the impatient

You have a 42-key Corne with MX hot-swap sockets. Here is the one-sentence answer:

**Buy a uniform-profile PBT set (DSA or XDA), 60+ keys, from Meckeys or Stickeys. Spend ₹1,500–3,500. Stop there.**

The rest of this doc is the evidence and alternatives for when you want to go deeper.

## 1. The One Thing That Matters Most on a Corne

### Uniform profile is mandatory

The Corne's columnar-stagger layout has no concept of "rows" the way a standard keyboard does. The index finger hits what would be a mid-row key. The pinky reaches for what would be a top-row key. Your three thumb keys sit below everything. And because you use ZMK layers, the key at position X might be `J` on the base layer and `cursor-left` on NAV layer.

**Sculpted profiles (Cherry, OEM, SA) have different angles and heights per row.** If you put a Row 1 keycap in a position your finger treats as Row 3, it feels tilted and strange. You will notice this. It is annoying.

**Uniform profiles (DSA, XDA, KAT, KAM, PBS) are all the same height.** Any key goes anywhere. Remap freely, move keys around between layers, hand a friend a key from a different set — it all works identically. This is not optional for a Corne. It is the single most important purchasing decision.

### What this disqualifies immediately

These are popular, well-made keycap sets that are **wrong for your Corne**:

| Set | Why it's wrong |
|---|---|
| HK Gaming Cherry profile (Amazon.in) | Cherry profile = sculpted |
| Keychron Double Shot OSA/OEM sets | OSA and OEM = sculpted |
| Tai-Hao OEM/Cubic profile sets | OEM/Cubic = sculpted |
| Akko Ocean Star (SAL profile) | SAL = sculpted (R4/R4/R3/R3/R3/R3) |
| Any "Cherry profile PBT" set | Cherry = sculpted |
| SA profile anything | Too tall, too loud, sculpted |

The Akko Ocean Star is particularly tricky — it looks great, it's available on Amazon.in, and Akko is otherwise a trusted brand. But **SAL is a sculpted profile**. Do not buy it for the Corne.

### All 1u is your advantage

The Corne uses only 1u keys. No 1.25u, no 1.5u, no spacebars. Any keycap set with 60+ keys will give you more than enough 1u keys for all 42 positions. You will have spare keys left over. Do not worry about 1u coverage — it is the one Corne-specific concern that is already solved.

## 2. What Will Feel Best — The Honest Ranking

This is subjective, but there is a clear community consensus for ergo and 40% boards:

### Profile ranking for Corne (best feel first)

**1. PBS (Penguin Belly Slide)** — Currently the best uniform profile for Corne users, per community consensus. 7.5mm tall, hybrid cylindrical+spherical scoop, 1.5mm thick PBT walls. Users consistently prefer it over DSA for acoustics and fingertip contact. The thick PBT makes it sound noticeably deeper than DSA. *Downside: Very hard to find in India. Needs international order from CannonKeys or Yuzu Keycaps.*

**2. DSA** — The classic Corne/40% choice. 7.6mm, spherical top, uniform. Every position feels the same. The slightly grainy PBT texture on good DSA sets is genuinely pleasant after a few days. Widely available. Easy first purchase. *Downside: Some cheaper DSA is thinner (1.4mm) and sounds clackier.*

**3. XDA** — Slightly taller than DSA (9mm), flat top with mild spherical scoop. More finger contact area — some people find this more comfortable for long sessions. Very available in India. *Downside: Flat top means slightly less fingertip guidance than DSA's spherical dish.*

**4. KAT** — Medium height (10mm), uniform, spherical top. A nice balance between DSA and SA — more key to grab than DSA, less fatiguing than SA. Good community reception. *Downside: Limited India availability, usually needs import.*

**5. KAM** — Explicitly recommended in the ergo community for boards like Corne, Kyria, Lily58. 9.05mm, spherical, uniform. *Downside: Very hard to find in India.*

**6. MT3 (uniform variant)** — Deep, aggressive spherical dish. Retro feel. Some people love it, many find it fatiguing. *Downside: Mostly ABS, tall, hard to find in India.*

**7. Blank keycaps** — Not a profile, but a choice worth ranking. Many Corne users run pure blanks. More on this in Section 4.

### Material feel

PBT beats ABS for a Corne. You type on these all day. PBT's slightly matte, grainy surface does not slip under sweaty fingers. ABS gets shiny within a few months of heavy use. Both are fine initially, but PBT is the answer at every price point for long-term builds.

Thick PBT (1.5mm wall, not 1.2mm) sounds noticeably better — deeper, less hollow. This is the difference between a cheap DSA set and a quality one at the same price. When the listing says "thick PBT" or "1.5mm," that is the one to pick.

## 4. The Blank Keycap Case

Many Corne users, after spending weeks or months on the board, remove all their legend keycaps and go fully blank. Here is why this makes sense:

**42 keys is memorizable.** Your Corne has fewer keys than most people's phones. You already know where HJKL is on your base layer. Legends become irrelevant faster than you expect.

**Legends lie on the NAV layer.** The `J` key (which you know by muscle memory) sends `cursor-down` when you hold your NAV thumb key. The legend says `J`. The reality is `↓`. Every key with a legend is telling you the wrong thing for every layer that isn't base. Blanks sidestep this entirely.

**Blank PBT is cheaper.** A blank DSA set costs less than the same set with dye-sub legends. You pay extra for information you will stop reading within weeks.

**It looks cleaner.** A fully blank board in a single colorway is visually coherent in a way that a QWERTY-labeled board is not.

**The specific recommendation for blanks in India:**
- Meckeys blank DSA 1u: ₹100/key × 42 = ₹4,200 (mix colors freely)
- YMDK DSA Blank 61-key set on Amazon.in: ₹1,500–2,500 (one colorway, complete)
- splitted.space ships MX DSA blanks internationally with India shipping (~€25 for a grey set)

## 7. Importing from International Vendors

When Indian stock isn't enough — or when you want something specific like PBS or a particular DSA colorway.

### The Landed Cost Reality

Import into India has two main charges:
- **Basic Customs Duty (BCD)**: ~15% on HS code 39269099 (plastic keycaps)
- **IGST**: 18% on (CIF value + BCD)

**Example calculation for a $35 USD keycap set:**
```
Product:           $35    = ₹2,900
Shipping (FedEx):  $15    = ₹1,250
CIF total:                = ₹4,150
BCD (15%):                = ₹623
IGST (18% on CIF+BCD):    = ₹863
Total landed:             ₹5,636
```
A $35 set realistically costs ₹5,600–6,500 landed. Budget accordingly.

**The AliExpress exception**: Many AliExpress sellers under-declare value on the customs form. Packages often arrive duty-free. This is technically the buyer's risk, not a guarantee. Small packages (<₹5,000 declared) often clear without duty in practice. Higher-value packages from branded vendors (KBDfans, CannonKeys) are declared honestly and will be assessed.

## 9. What to Avoid

### Wrong profiles (the most expensive mistake)

- **OEM profile**: Comes with most gaming keyboards. Sculpted. Wrong for Corne.
- **Cherry profile**: The enthusiast favourite for standard boards. Sculpted. Wrong for Corne.
- **SA profile**: Beautiful, loud, extremely tall, sculpted. Wrong for Corne on every axis.
- **Akko SAL profile**: Looks uniform-adjacent but is sculpted (R4/R4/R3/R3/R3/R3). Wrong for Corne.
- **Keychron OEM profile**: Keychron's basic keycap sets ship in OEM. Wrong for Corne.

### Materials/construction to skip

- **Thin ABS (1.2mm or less)**: Sounds hollow. Gets shiny within months. Stock gaming keyboard keycaps.
- **Laser-etched legends**: The laser chars the surface of the keycap. Legends fade in 6–12 months of heavy use. Look for "dye-sublimation" or "double-shot" instead.
- **"UV print" or "pad print" legends**: Also fade. Not as bad as laser etching but not durable.

### Sets without enough 1u keys

Your Corne needs 42 × 1u. This is almost never a problem — any 60-key or larger set covers it. But small "gaming accent" packs (like Tai-Hao's 18-key or 42-key rubber packs) may be undersized or wrong profile. Check the key count before ordering small packs.

### Cheap "novelty" sets

₹600 import listings on AliExpress for 100+ keycap sets. Some are fine. Many are thin-walled ABS that sound like plastic cups. If a listing doesn't specify PBT and doesn't give a wall thickness, assume the worst.

## 10. Care Once You Have Them

**Cleaning**: The gentle way is a keycap puller (or the wire one that came with your hot-swap kit), remove all 42, soak in warm water + a drop of dish soap for 20 minutes, swirl, rinse, dry face-down on a microfiber cloth for a few hours. Never boiling water (PBT can warp). Never dishwasher. Never acetone.

**Preventing shine on ABS**: You can't. If you're running ABS, the shine is coming. Some people like the worn-in look ("patina"). If you hate it, run PBT.

**Storage**: Store spare sets in the plastic trays or zip-lock bags they came in. Keycaps left loose in a drawer collect dust and oil. Label the bags if you have multiple sets.

**Replacing a single key**: If you lose or break one of your 42, the spare keys from your original set save you. Keep the spare keys from any set you buy. For rare colours, Meckeys sells individual DSA 1u keys — go to their website and order exactly the one you need.

**RGB with PBT**: Most PBT dye-sub sets will block or diffuse RGB. The Corne's per-key LEDs will glow around the edges of the keycap rather than through it. If RGB bleed matters to you, choose shine-through (ABS/PC double-shot) keycaps and accept the shine tradeoff. For a no-LED or underglow-only build (common on nice!nano wireless builds), this is irrelevant.
