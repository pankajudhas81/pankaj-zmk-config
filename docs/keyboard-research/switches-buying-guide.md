> **This doc is about what to actually buy and where.** Theory (switch anatomy, linear/tactile/clicky mechanics, lubing chemistry) lives in [switches-research.md](./switches-research.md). Read that first if you want the foundations. This doc assumes you know the difference between a linear and a tactile and just want to make a purchase.

## TL;DR for the impatient

You have 42 keys, MX hot-swap sockets, and the freedom to change every switch in 20 minutes. You need 47 switches minimum (42 + 5 spares).

**Fastest purchase path:**
- Want smooth and light: buy **Gateron Yellow** from Meckeys — ₹160/10, total ₹752 for 47. Done.

## 1. The Corne Advantage

Most people who buy a mechanical keyboard are stuck with its switches forever. The switches are soldered to the PCB. Changing them means desoldering 60–104 joints — a multi-hour job that risks damaging the board.

You do not have this problem. Your Corne has Kailh MX hot-swap sockets. Every switch pushes in and pulls out bare-handed (or with a cheap switch puller). Swapping all 42 switches takes about 20 minutes. This fundamentally changes how you should think about switches:

**Switches are a collection, not a permanent decision.** Buy a set, use it for a week, decide you want something different, buy another set, swap in 20 minutes. The first set goes into a small bag for next time. After a few months you'll have three or four sets and a strong opinion about what you actually prefer.

You need **47 switches minimum**: 42 for every key position, plus 5 spares for travel damage, misplaced switches, or that one key that just feels slightly off and needs swapping. Most switches are sold in packs of 10, 35, 45, 65, or 70. Plan your order around these pack sizes — buying 50 is usually cheaper than buying 47 individually.

## 2. What Feel Are You After?

Use this decision tree before spending money. Pick the branch that matches what you want, ignore the rest.

```
Do you want to hear/feel something when you press a key?
│
├── NO → You want SMOOTH and QUIET
│   ├── Silent matters most → Gazzew Boba LT or Gateron Silent Red
│   └── Silent is optional → Gateron Yellow (35gf) or Gateron Oil King (55gf)
│
└── YES → You want FEEDBACK
    ├── Audible click matters → CLICKY
    │   ├── Crisp click, daily use → Kailh Box White
    │   └── Loud, satisfying, not office-safe → Kailh Box Jade
    │
    └── Just a tactile bump, no click → TACTILE
        ├── Quiet tactile → Boba U4 (silent) — needs import
        └── Normal tactile
            ├── Budget, India stock → Akko CS Jelly Purple or Gateron Brown
            └── Best feel, willing to import → Boba U4T
```

**For a Corne specifically: tactile switches are unusually popular.** Here is why. You are using layers constantly — NAV, NUM, SYM. Your thumb holds a layer key while your other fingers press the actual key. Without looking down, the bump in a tactile switch confirms "yes, I pressed that key" without bottoming out. On a linear switch, you feel nothing until you bottom out, which encourages hammering keys rather than light, precise taps. The bump trains lighter fingers. Most experienced Corne users end up on tactiles.

That said: if you come from a gaming background and prefer fast, light keypresses, linear is completely valid. It is a preference, not a mistake.

### 1. Gateron Yellow (standard 3-pin)
**Type:** Linear · **Actuation:** 35 gf · **Travel:** 4.0mm

The best budget linear in the world, and it happens to be available in India. Extremely smooth for the price, lighter than Cherry MX Red, almost no scratchiness. The 35gf weight means it registers with minimal force — good for fast layer switching, slightly risky for mistyping until your muscle memory locks in.

- **Why for a Corne:** The light actuation suits rapid thumb movements on layer keys. If you're swapping out switches for the first time and not sure what you want, start here — low cost to experiment.
- **Price per switch:** ₹16 (₹160 per 10-pack at Meckeys)
- **Cost for 47:** ₹752 (five 10-packs)
- **Where to buy:** [Meckeys.com](https://meckeys.com/shop/accessories/keyboard-accessories/key-switches/gateron-mechanical-switch/) — search "Gateron Mechanical Switch 3-pin Yellow". Also available on Amazon.in via MECKEYS seller, ASIN [B0CCVF2CQF](https://www.amazon.in/MECKEYS-Gateron-Mechanical-Switch-Brown/dp/B0CCVF2CQF) (pack of 10, select Yellow variant).
- **Honest downside:** Stock (unlubed) is slightly scratchy. A 30-minute lube session transforms them. At ₹752 for the whole board, even adding ₹2,200 lube still totals under ₹3,000 for a world-class linear setup.

### 3. Akko CS Jelly Purple
**Type:** Tactile · **Actuation:** 40 gf · **Travel:** 3.5mm

The best budget tactile you can buy in India without importing anything. A subtle-to-medium bump, light actuation, and Akko's dustproof stem design. The Jelly series is an upgrade over the standard Lavender Purple — tighter housing tolerances, better out-of-box feel. Available on Amazon.in.

- **Why for a Corne:** Layer-heavy typing benefits from any tactile feedback. This is the lowest-cost way to add it. 40gf is light enough not to fatigue the pinky on outlying keys.
- **Price per switch:** ~₹30–35 (45-pack for ~₹1,400–1,600 on Amazon.in)
- **Cost for 47:** ~₹1,400–1,650 (one 45-pack + a few extras, or a 55-pack if available)

### 5. Kailh Box Jade
**Type:** Clicky (click bar) · **Actuation:** 50 gf · **Travel:** 3.6mm

The best clicky switch for typing. Box switches use a click bar mechanism (not a click jacket like Cherry Blue) — the click fires both on press and release, giving a distinctive double-click sound. Box design also means the stem is IP56-rated waterproof and dustproof. The Jade has a thicker click bar than Box White, making it louder and more tactile-feeling.

If the Jade is too loud, the **Kailh Box White** is the same mechanism with a thinner bar: quieter, lighter click. Both available at Meckeys.

- **Why for a Corne:** If you type alone and want auditory confirmation of every keypress, clicky is a legitimate choice. The click on a Box switch is extremely consistent — you'll never miss whether you actuated. Also: the box housing protects against Bangalore dust.
- **Price per switch:** ₹27 (₹270 per 10-pack at Meckeys) for Jade. Box V2 variants at ₹32.50/switch (₹325/10).
- **Cost for 47:** ₹1,269 (Jade) or ₹1,528 (Box V2)
- **Where to buy:** [Meckeys.com — Kailh Box Jade](https://meckeys.com/shop/accessories/keyboard-accessories/key-switches/kailh-box-jade-switch/). Box variants also on Amazon.in: Jade 65pcs ASIN [B0841HQFCL](https://www.amazon.in/Kailh-Switches-Mechanical-Gaming-Keyboards/dp/B0841HQFCL), Box 65pcs ASIN [B0841H5RVY](https://www.amazon.in/Kailh-Switches-Mechanical-Gaming-Keyboards/dp/B0841H5RVY).
- **Honest downside:** **Not office-safe, not coworker-safe, not family-safe.** The Box Jade is loud. You will be asked to stop. Also: Corne users on shared calls should have a secondary silent set ready to swap in. Clicky is a weekend-at-home switch.

## 4. Switch Testers — Try Before You Buy 47

Buying a switch tester is the single best ₹500 you can spend before committing to 47 switches. A tester is a small board — typically 9 to 18 positions — each with a different switch type installed. You type on them, one at a time, and discover which feel you actually prefer rather than which you think you prefer from a description.

**The rule:** Type on each switch for at least 5 minutes, not 5 seconds. Your first impression is almost always wrong. The switch that feels "nice" at first often reveals a flaw after 50 keystrokes. The switch you dismiss in 5 seconds often reveals a pleasant subtlety after real typing.

## 7. Importing When India Doesn't Have What You Want

Some switches — particularly the Boba U4T, Gazzew U4 (silent tactile), Durock Koala, premium Gateron Ink V2, and Durock L7 — are not reliably stocked in India. If you want these, you're importing.

### The landed cost reality

Import into India adds two charges on top of product + shipping:
- **Basic Customs Duty (BCD):** ~15% on declared value
- **IGST:** 18% on (declared value + BCD)
- Combined effective rate: ~35–36% on top of CIF (cost + insurance + freight)

**Example: importing 47 × Boba U4T from Divinikey (US)**
```
47 switches × $0.70/switch:     $32.90  ≈  ₹2,740
Shipping (USPS International):  $18.00  ≈  ₹1,500
CIF total:                              ≈  ₹4,240
BCD (15%):                              ≈  ₹636
IGST (18% on ₹4,876):                   ≈  ₹878
────────────────────────────────────────────────
Total landed (47 switches):             ≈  ₹5,754
Per switch:                             ≈  ₹122
```

That is expensive. But Boba U4T is genuinely not available at a reasonable India price today, and it is consistently ranked among the best tactiles in the world for split ergo boards. If you decide you want it, the import math is the only path.

### Recommended international vendors

**NovelKeys (novelkeys.com)**
- Curated selection. Stocks Gazzew/Boba switches reliably. Ships internationally; taxes and duties charged at checkout so no surprise on delivery.
- Good for: Boba U4T, Boba U4 (silent tactile), niche switches not found elsewhere.
- Shipping to India: ~$15–25 depending on weight.

**Divinikey (divinikey.com)**
- US-based, transparent pricing, ships internationally. No hidden tariffs after checkout.
- Good for: Boba U4T, Gateron Ink variants, Durock switches.
- Shipping: ~$15–20 to India.

**KBDfans (kbdfans.com)**
- Large Chinese vendor. Wide selection, competitive prices, ships via JCEX (3–5 days) or ePacket (15–20 days for orders under $130).
- Good for: Durock L7, Gateron Ink V2, budget options, group buy access.

**AliExpress**
- Cheapest total cost if the package clears customs without duty assessment (common for small packages declared under ₹5,000). Not a guarantee, just how it often works in practice.
- Risk: Variable quality, longer wait (15–30 days), no easy returns.

### When is importing worth it?

Import if: the switch you want cannot be found in India AND the landed price is comparable to what a local equivalent would cost with lube added. Boba U4T at ₹122/switch imported vs. Gateron Brown at ₹16/switch local — the Brown is cheaper but the U4T is meaningfully better. If you've tried a tactile and want the best one, import it.

Don't import if: you're still figuring out whether you prefer linear or tactile. Buy local budget switches first, develop a preference, then import if needed.

## 8. Lubing Your New Switches

Stock switches come with inconsistent factory lube or none at all. Lubing them takes 2–3 hours for 47 switches and transforms the feel. It is not optional for linears if you want them to be good. It is worth it for tactiles if you have the time. Skip it entirely for clickies — lube destroys the click mechanism.

### What lube to buy

**For linears (Gateron Yellow, Oil King):** Krytox 205g0. Thick grease applied to stem sides, stem bottom, and switch housing rails. Makes a scratchy linear silky. Do not over-apply — thin coat only, or it gums up the spring.

**For tactiles (Akko Jelly Purple, Gateron Brown):** Tribosys 3204. Thinner than 205g0. Apply to housing rails and stem sides only. **Do not touch the tactile legs on the stem** — this kills the bump. 3204 is specifically formulated for tactiles because it won't over-dampen the feedback.

**For springs (all switches):** Bag-lube with Krytox 105 or Tribosys 3204. Put all springs in a small bag with a tiny amount of lube, shake, deploy. Eliminates spring ping.

### Where to get lube in India

**Stickeys.in:** Krytox 205g0 in 5g at ₹2,200. Sufficient for 100+ switches. Stock varies — check before ordering. https://stickeys.in/products/krytox-205go

**StacksKB.com:** Krytox GPL 205g0 available. Checks their current listings at https://stackskb.com/store/krytox-gpl-205g0/ — domestic India orders only (they won't ship lubricants internationally).

**NeoMacro.in:** Krytox 205g0 stocked. Useful backup.

**For Tribosys 3204 in India:** Harder to find locally. Check StacksKB first. If unavailable, include it in an international switch order (Divinikey and NovelKeys both stock it at ~$8–12 USD for a 2ml tube). 2ml is more than enough for 47 tactiles.

**Meckeys alternative:** Glorious G-Lube is available at Meckeys and on Amazon.in. It works and is cheaper than Krytox. Not as thick as 205g0 — more suitable for tactiles than linears. Fine for a first-time luber who doesn't want to spend ₹2,200 upfront.

### Time and cost investment

- **Time:** 2–3 hours for 47 switches if you're methodical. First time will be slower. Set up a small station: switch opener, brush, lube, music.
- **Cost:** ₹2,200 (Krytox 205g0, 5g) + ₹200–400 (switch opener) = ~₹2,400–2,600 upfront. The 5g lasts through several sets of switches over years.
- **Is it worth it:** Yes for linears — stock Gateron Yellow goes from "decent" to "excellent" with 20 minutes of work per switch. Optional for tactiles — Akko Jelly Purple is already acceptable stock. Skip entirely for Kailh Box clicky.

### YouTube tutorial

## 10. What to Avoid

**Cherry MX** — ₹55–70+/switch in India (Meckeys cited ₹55/switch in 2022; likely higher now). The Gateron G Pro 3.0 Yellow ships factory-lubed, feels smoother, and costs ₹20/switch. Cherry's quality advantage over Gateron disappeared around 2018. You're paying for the brand name. Not worth it.

**Generic "blue switches" or "red switches" from no-name brands** — Amazon.in has dozens of listings for unlabeled switches from brands like HUYUN, YINDIAO, or generic "RGB mechanical switch." These have no lineage, inconsistent quality between batches, and often misrepresent actuation specs. The price difference over real Gateron is negligible. Buy Gateron.

**Anything listed only as "RGB switches" without a manufacturer name** — If the listing says "RGB MX-compatible switches" and the brand name is the keyboard brand, not a switch manufacturer, those are no-name Outemu or Chinese commodity switches with RGB cutouts. Not necessarily bad, but not what you want for a custom build you're putting thought into.

**Optical switches** — Your Corne PCB has Kailh hot-swap sockets designed for **MX mechanical switches** with two metal contact pins. Optical switches use a different mechanism (a light beam interrupted by the stem) and have different pin configurations. They will not seat correctly in MX sockets and will not register. Gateron makes both optical and mechanical switches — make sure any Gateron listing says "mechanical" or "MX-compatible", not "optical."

**Low-profile Choc switches** — Kailh Choc is a completely different switch format with a different footprint, different stem shape, and different socket type. Your Corne uses MX sockets. Choc switches will not fit. Some Corne variants are designed for Choc (Corne LP) but yours is the standard MX version. Do not order Choc switches regardless of how good the reviews are.

**Low-profile MX switches** — Gateron and Kailh make low-profile MX switches (2mm travel vs 4mm). They use the same two-pin MX footprint and will physically fit in your sockets, but the travel is drastically shorter than standard MX and many people find them unsatisfying for deliberate typing. If you see "low profile" in the product name, confirm you actually want that.
