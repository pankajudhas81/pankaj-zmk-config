# Keyboard Switches — Complete Research Guide

Switches are the **mechanical heart** of every mechanical keyboard. Each key has one switch underneath it. When you press a key, the switch registers the input. Everything about how a keyboard feels and sounds comes primarily from the switches.

---

## Anatomy of a Switch

A mechanical switch has three main parts:

```
         ┌──────────────┐
         │     STEM     │  ← the moving part (color-coded by feel)
         │  (cruciform) │
         └──────┬───────┘
                │
         ┌──────▼───────┐
         │    HOUSING   │  ← top + bottom shell
         │  (top+base)  │
         └──────┬───────┘
                │
         ┌──────▼───────┐
         │    SPRING    │  ← determines weight/resistance
         └──────────────┘
```

- **Stem** — slides up and down; the cross-shaped bottom fits MX keycap stems. Its shape defines the switch type (linear, tactile, clicky).
- **Housing** — top and bottom casing. Material matters: nylon is deep and muted, polycarbonate is brighter and crisper.
- **Spring** — progressive or linear; heavier spring = more resistance. Measured in **gram-force (gf)** or **centinewtons (cN)** — same thing.

![Switch cross-section diagram](https://keyboard.university/img/switch-diagram.png)

> A great interactive reference: **keyboard.university** — covers everything from spring weights to lubing.

## The Three Switch Types

### 1. Linear
**Feel:** Smooth all the way down. No bump, no click. Just resistance that builds as the spring compresses.

**Who it's for:** Gamers (fast repeated keypresses), typists who want quiet, people who dislike feedback.

**Sound:** Thocky/clunky depending on housing. Can be made very quiet.

**Famous linears:**
| Switch | Actuation | Feel | Notes |
|--------|-----------|------|-------|
| Cherry MX Red | 45 gf | Smooth but scratchy stock | The classic. Not the best anymore. |
| Gateron Yellow | 35 gf | Very smooth | Best budget linear. |
| Gateron G Pro 3.0 Yellow | 35 gf | Factory lubed, smooth | Excellent value. |
| Gateron Milky Yellow Pro | 35 gf | Slightly muted sound | Popular for thock. |
| Akko CS Jelly series | 45 gf | Smooth, affordable | CS Jelly Pink, Black, etc. |
| Kailh Speed Silver | 40 gf | Very short travel | Gaming-focused. |
| Boba U4T (linear mod) | — | — | Actually tactile; listed for comparison. |
| Durock L7 | 67 gf | Smooth, heavy | Popular endgame option. |
| Gateron Oil King | 55 gf | Very smooth, deep sound | Top-tier linear. |
| Gateron Black Ink V2 | 60 gf | Smooth, poppy | Premium feel. |
| Haimu Heartbeat | 45 gf | Smooth + magnetic feel | Unique magnetic spring. |

---

### 2. Tactile
**Feel:** A noticeable bump partway through the keypress. You feel when the switch actuates. No click sound from a leaf spring — just the bump.

**Who it's for:** Typists who want feedback without noise. Office-friendly but satisfying.

**Sound:** Can be quiet (silent tactiles) or have a pronounced "thock" on the bump.

**Famous tactiles:**
| Switch | Actuation | Bump | Notes |
|--------|-----------|------|-------|
| Cherry MX Brown | 45 gf | Very mild | The most common. Considered underwhelming by enthusiasts — "scratchy red with a hint of bump." |
| Glorious Panda | 67 gf | Strong, early | Beloved. Holy Panda DNA. |
| Boba U4 | 62 gf | Medium, quiet | Best silent tactile. |
| Boba U4T | 62 gf | Medium, louder | Non-silent version of U4. |
| Topre 45g | 45 gf | Unique rubber dome | See Topre section below — different tech entirely. |
| Gateron Brown | 45 gf | Mild | Better than Cherry Brown, smoother. |
| Akko CS Lavender Purple | 43 gf | Light bump | Good budget tactile. |
| Durock Koala | 67 gf | Strong bump | Popular in the community. |
| Marshmallow | 45 gf | Light bump | Smooth and quiet. |

---

### 3. Clicky
**Feel:** A tactile bump PLUS an audible click sound from a physical click mechanism (bar or jacket). Two subtypes:

- **Click bar** (e.g., Kailh Box switches) — click sound on both press AND release
- **Click jacket** (e.g., Cherry MX Blue) — click only on press

**Who it's for:** Typists who love auditory feedback. **Not office-friendly.**

**Famous clickies:**
| Switch | Actuation | Sound | Notes |
|--------|-----------|-------|-------|
| Cherry MX Blue | 50 gf | Classic click | The iconic clicky. Loud, satisfying to some. |
| Kailh Box White | 45 gf | Crisp click | Click bar — very crisp, consistent. |
| Kailh Box Jade | 50 gf | Loud, clicky | Thicker click bar = louder click. Popular. |
| Kailh Box Navy | 60 gf | Very loud | Heavier version of Jade. For the bold. |
| Gateron Blue | 50 gf | Click jacket | Smoother than Cherry Blue. |
| Razer Green | 50 gf | Click jacket | Cherry Blue clone, slightly different tuning. |

## Switch Specifications Explained

```
Actuation Force: 45 gf         ← how hard you press to register a keypress
Pre-travel:      2.0 mm        ← distance before actuation point
Total Travel:    4.0 mm        ← full distance to bottom out
Reset point:     1.5 mm        ← how far up before keypress resets (important for fast typing)
```

**Key things to know:**
- **Light switches (35–45 gf):** Fast, less fatigue. Can cause accidental keypresses.
- **Medium switches (50–62 gf):** The sweet spot for most typists.
- **Heavy switches (65+ gf):** Deliberate press required. Great for reducing typos.
- **Short travel (speed switches):** 3.4–3.5mm total. Good for gaming, odd for typing.
- **Long travel (ALPS, Topre):** Different feel entirely.

## Beyond MX: Other Switch Technologies

### Topre (Electrocapacitive Rubber Dome)
Not mechanical in the traditional sense. A rubber dome sits over a spring and a PCB sensor. When you compress the dome, the capacitance changes and registers the keypress. The result is a unique "thock" sound and a smooth, pillowy feel unlike anything else.

- **Used in:** Happy Hacking Keyboard (HHKB), Realforce, Leopold FC660C
- **Feel:** Buttery smooth, unique; devotees call it "endgame"
- **Weight:** 35g, 45g, 55g variants
- **Downside:** Expensive, proprietary keycaps, not hot-swap compatible typically

![HHKB Professional Hybrid](https://hhkeyboard.us/img/products/hybrid/hhkb-hybrid-hero.png)

---

### Hall Effect (Magnetic)
Uses a Hall Effect sensor to detect a magnet in the stem — no physical contact, so theoretically infinite lifespan. Also allows **analog input** (partial press = partial movement, like a joystick).

- **Used in:** Wooting keyboards, Aimpad, SteelSeries (some), newer gaming boards
- **Feature:** Rapid Trigger — actuation point can be set per-key down to 0.1mm. Dominant in competitive gaming right now.
- **Popular boards:** Wooting 60HE, Wooting Two HE, Razer Huntsman V3

![Wooting Hall Effect diagram](https://wooting.io/img/he-explanation.png)

---

### Optical
A light beam registers the keypress when the stem interrupts it — no metal contact. Fast registration (shorter actuation), very consistent.

- **Used in:** Razer Huntsman, Corsair OPX, Logitech GX series
- **Pros:** Fast, consistent, long lifespan
- **Cons:** Less choice in switches, proprietary

---

### Alps / Matias
Alps switches (American Linear Products / Alps Electric) have a completely different housing and stem shape than MX. Older standard, found in vintage boards (Apple Extended Keyboard II uses ALPS). Matias makes modern reproductions.

- **Famous Alps:** SKCM Brown (tactile), SKCM Blue (clicky), SKCM Orange
- **Feel:** Scratchy but very distinctive; adored by vintage enthusiasts

## Hot-Swap vs Soldered

**Hot-swap:** PCB has sockets. You push switches in and pull them out with no soldering. Change switches anytime.

**Soldered:** Switches are soldered to the PCB. Permanent unless you desolder.

If you're new, **always get hot-swap**. You'll want to try different switches.

---

## Lubing Switches

Stock switches often have minor friction or inconsistency. Lubing improves feel and sound dramatically.

- **Linear switches:** Krytox 205g0 on stem sides and bottom housing. Makes them silky.
- **Tactile switches:** Light lube only on legs and housing — NOT on the tactile legs or you'll kill the bump.
- **Clicky switches:** Generally don't lube — you'll destroy the click mechanism.
- **Springs:** Bag lube with Krytox 105 or Tribosys 3204 to eliminate spring ping.

Films (thin pieces of material between top and bottom housing) can also tighten housing tolerance and change sound.

## Switch Testers — Try Before You Commit

Buy a switch tester before committing to 42–104 switches. Common testers:

- **Gateron switch tester** (9 or 18 key, ~$10–15)
- **Kailh switch tester** (Box switches, ~$15)
- **NovelKeys tester packs** — curated sets of popular switches
- **TheKeyBlog / vendor samplers** — buy 10 of different types

> Rule of thumb: type on a switch for 5 minutes, not 5 seconds. Your opinion will change.

## How Many Switches Do You Need?

| Keyboard Size | Switch Count |
|---------------|-------------|
| Full size (100%) | ~104 |
| TKL (80%) | ~87 |
| 75% | ~84 |
| 65% | ~68 |
| 60% | ~61 |
| 40% (like your Corne) | ~42 |

Always buy 5–10 extra for spares.
